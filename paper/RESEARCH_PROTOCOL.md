# DataSentry AI research protocol and completion record

## Question and hypotheses

Across three small tabular regression tasks, does a fixed, transparent policy that selects a cleaning recipe from observed training features improve untouched held-out RMSE over a fixed median/mode recipe under controlled training-feature corruption?

- H1: More corruption generally increases held-out error, with variation by dataset and model.
- H2: The policy improves paired RMSE in some conditions, without an assumption that it wins universally.
- H3: IQR clipping can hurt when flagged extremes are valid or the cap distorts useful predictors.

This is an empirical evaluation of a simple heuristic. It does not claim a novel general data-cleaning algorithm.

## Frozen study design

The three official UCI-derived datasets are Portuguese-course Student Performance (`G3`, excluding later grades `G1`, `G2`, and `absences` for early prediction), red Wine Quality (`quality`), and Energy Efficiency (`heating_load`; `cooling_load` is excluded). Sources and exact transformation choices are recorded in [../data/README.md](../data/README.md).

Five seeded 80/20 outer splits group identical feature rows. Each split uses one corruption mask for every competing arm. The interventions modify training features only: random missingness (MCAR-like), feature-dependent missingness (MAR-like), and numeric extremes, each at 0%, 5%, 15%, and 30% of eligible cells. Test features and targets remain untouched. The target is never corrupted. Synthetic affected cells are known perturbations; natural outlier flags do not prove an error.

The four prespecified recipes are median/mode, KNN numeric imputation, median/mode plus 1.5-IQR clipping, and a frozen training-feature rule choosing among these recipes. Ridge regression, a 100-tree random forest, and a small multilayer perceptron use fixed settings. Imputation, encoding, scaling, clipping thresholds, rule decisions, and estimators are fit on the training partition only. At 0% corruption, the median arm is the uncorrupted reference with the processing required by the estimators. At positive missingness, it is the minimally repaired baseline.

Primary metric: held-out RMSE and paired RMSE difference from fixed median/mode. Secondary outputs: MAE, R², runtime, affected-cell count, split and corruption-mask hashes. Results are reported by dataset, mechanism, level, and model. Seeds are repeated evaluations of each dataset rather than independent new datasets. No model or policy choice uses the outer test scores.

## Completed evidence

The committed [results](results/) contain 720 run-level rows and a source/configuration manifest for each dataset: 2,160 fitted arms in total. [study_summary.csv](study_summary.csv) and the two vector figures are generated by `experiments/summarize_study.py`, which validates the expected grid before plotting. The policy's RMSE is lower than median/mode in 31 of 81 positive-corruption condition means, equal in 36, and higher in 14. This mixed outcome, model-specific harms, and synthetic-corruption limitations are discussed in the paper. The optional app-only duplicate and category-normalization comparisons are exploratory and not mixed into these paper counts.
